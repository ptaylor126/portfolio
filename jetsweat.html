01 Overview

Redesigning a fitness studio dashboard.

JetSweat is a B2B SaaS platform for fitness studios. I worked on it as a freelance product designer for about 3 months. The main piece of work was a redesign of the studio dashboard. I also designed a free trial onboarding flow and a self-serve website builder. The designs were not built.

02 The Old Dashboard

The old dashboard was hard to scan.

JetSweat already had an operational dashboard, but it didn't show the important numbers at a glance. I started with a heuristic review. Three problems stood out. The menu had too many items and no grouping. Nothing on the page told you where to look first. And revenue was split across separate cards, with no trend or context.

Too many menu items Unclear organization and no grouping
No scanning hierarchy Key metrics don't stand out
Revenue data split up Numbers without context or trends
03 Dashboard Redesign

The redesigned dashboard.

I redesigned it so studio owners could see what matters first and act on it from the same screen. I also grouped the navigation so there are fewer top-level items.

04 How the Dashboard Works

How the dashboard works.

Key numbers in one row.

The four numbers studio owners check first sit in one row at the top: users, subscribers, cancellations and one-time purchases. You read them left to right before anything else.

Change badges.

Each number has a badge that shows which way it moved and by how much. You don't need to open a chart to see it.

Revenue in one chart.

Total revenue and non-subscription revenue sit on the same trend chart. You compare them in one place, instead of across separate cards.

Date filter.

One control switches every number between 1 day, 7 days, 14 days and 1 month.

Actions next to the data.

Studio owners can start a class from the upcoming livestreams card. They can create a video or a livestream from the top bar.

05 Free Trial

A free trial studios can start on their own.

Studios couldn't try the platform before buying. JetSweat wanted to become a product-led growth company. So I studied 9 platforms to see how they handle product-led growth, onboarding and self-serve tools.

Then I designed a 5-step onboarding flow from scratch. Easy questions come first to build confidence, with personal and business details later. Studios could try the platform on their own, without the sales team.

Decision
Easy questions first.
Why
Light questions come first, like how people heard about JetSweat. Business details come later, once people are invested.
06 Site Builder

A website builder studios can use themselves.

Every new customer needed 3 hours of website setup by the ops team, done by hand. I designed a self-serve website builder with form fields and drag-and-drop uploads. A visual preview showed how content would look on the final site.

Simple form fields Studios edit content by filling in fields. No technical skills needed.
Logical sections Content is grouped into clear sections, so setup is easy to follow.
Visual reference A template shows where each piece of content will appear on the site.
Drag and drop uploads Uploading works the way people expect, so there's little to learn.
Professional output Studios get a professional-looking site without needing a designer.
Decision
Live preview.
Why
Studios see their content on the page as they build, so they don't have to guess how the site will look. The aim was fewer revisions and support tickets.

08 What I'd Change

What I'd change now.

Colour should follow good or bad.

Cancellations fell 24%, but the badge is red. Fewer cancellations is good news, so it should be green. Badge colour should show whether a change is good or bad, whichever way the number moves. The Views chart has the same problem. It uses red as the colour for the Livestream category, which reads like a warning.

Use a stacked bar for the user breakdown.

The User breakdown gauge would be clearer as a stacked bar. A bar makes the three groups easier to compare: once subscribed, never subscribed and subscribed.

09 Reflection

Next time, I'd talk to users.

There was no way to reach studio owners on this project. Next time I'd ask for even two or three, for half an hour each, before the designs were final.
